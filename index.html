Review & Explanations

What is pH?

The term pH stands for "potential of Hydrogen" (or power of hydrogen). It provides a compact logarithmic scale representing the chemical activity of solvated hydrogen ions in aqueous solution:

pH = -log₁₀[H₃O⁺]

Autoionization of Water

Even pure water spontaneously dissociates to a tiny extent into hydronium and hydroxide ions:

2H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + OH⁻
Kw = [H₃O⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴

At 25°C, [H₃O⁺] = [OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ M, producing a neutral pH of 7.00.

The Logarithmic Principle

Because pH is a base-10 logarithmic scale, a change of 1 pH unit corresponds to a 10-fold change in acidity:

  • • pH 4 is 10× more acidic than pH 5
  • • pH 3 is 100× more acidic than pH 5
  • • pH 2 is 1,000× more acidic than pH 5

Buffer Solutions

Buffers are solutions that resist changes in pH upon the addition of small amounts of strong acid or base. They typically consist of a weak conjugate acid-base pair, described by the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation:

pH = pKa + log₁₀([A⁻] / [HA])

Strong vs Weak Acids

Strong Acids (e.g., HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄) dissociate completely in water (100% ionization).

Weak Acids (e.g., CH₃COOH, HF) only partially ionize, establishing dynamic equilibrium governed by their acid dissociation constant (Ka).

Chemical Indicators

Acid-base indicators are weak organic dyes (HIn) whose protonated and deprotonated forms absorb different wavelengths of visible light:

HIn ⇌ H⁺ + In⁻

Color transitions typically occur across an interval of pH = pKIn ± 1.
